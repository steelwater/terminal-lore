## Character and personal history

Ada likes the moment when raw material acquires a defensible number. She believes a reliable grade begins long before a grading desk: it starts with a refinery batch whose origin, parameters, and losses can all be explained. She speaks plainly because a complicated sentence can conceal a missing measurement.

Her flaw is that a correct process can become more important to her than the colleague waiting for the output. She sometimes requests one more reconciliation when the existing batch record already supports release. She is not humorless, but her best jokes arrive so dryly that the room misses them until she has returned to work.

### Off-duty interests and family

Keeps an evolving collection of family cooking recipes written as process diagrams with margins full of corrections. An older cousin helped her into a first technical placement; the sense of being given an opportunity makes her exacting with her own work.

## Working relationships

Ada respects the standards maintained at Facet's grading desk, though a downgrade without an explanation can test her patience. She wants to reconcile the refinery numbers before a disputed batch proceeds.

## Operational assessment
