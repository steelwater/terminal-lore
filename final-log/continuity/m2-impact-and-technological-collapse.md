# M2 Impact and Technological Collapse

The M2 catastrophe is the event in which fragments associated with Horrox's second moon reached the inner Solar System and struck Earth, triggering a worldwide technological collapse.

This event is especially important as a continuity anchor for the **Runic Labyrinth** game.

> **NULL ARCHIVIST // CHRONOLOGY WARNING**  
> The event itself is strongly represented in recovered records, but the exact dates and physical explanations remain dependent on sources that do not always agree with later reconstructions. The sequence below preserves one major recovered chronology without declaring every year mechanically exact.

## Recovered Sequence

### 2076-08-23 — M2 Leaves Its Stable Orbit

M2's orbital speed around Horrox began accelerating. After several rotations, it was reported to have reached a velocity sufficient to leave the system on a course toward Earth.

### 2076-08-31 — Warning From Viajero

Viajero re-established contact from the Horrox orbital station and warned Earth about M2's movement and projected trajectory.

### 2078-01-01 — Earth Launches Interceptors

Earth launched missiles to intercept M2 before it could reach the planet.

### 2081-06-05 — M2 Breaks Apart

Before the interception could succeed, M2 broke into smaller pieces. Most of the original missiles missed their intended targets.

Earth prepared nuclear weapons for area detonations intended to deflect the fragments rather than strike each one directly.

### 2082-03-09 — Nuclear Deflection Attempt

The nuclear weapons detonated near the approaching fragments.

Some fragments were pushed toward Mars or Venus. Others missed the planets. A remaining group continued toward Earth.

### 2083 — The Impacts

Meteor fragments struck Earth over roughly two months. Some penetrated deep into the crust.

The recovered timeline attributes the following consequences to the impacts:

- widespread geological disturbance
- irregular volcanic eruptions across the world
- more than a year before worldwide volcanic activity subsided
- a global electromagnetic event severe enough to destroy electronics and technological systems
- collapse of communications, computing, infrastructure, and machine-dependent institutions

## The New Dark Age

The technological failure did not end with the initial disaster.

The surviving record says that some remaining technology was later broken or deliberately destroyed by militias and other groups who feared allowing rivals to possess a technological advantage.

Civilization therefore suffered both a **physical technological collapse** and a **social suppression of surviving technology**.

The result was a period remembered as a new dark age.

## Recovery

Humanity did not lose all accumulated knowledge.

Books and other durable records preserved enough scientific and technical understanding for electronics and computing to be rebuilt.

One recovered chronology records the creation of an **8086B** chip in 2113, followed by later handheld computers such as the Terminal 934 Model.

This means the post-impact world is not a return to prehistory. It is a civilization rebuilding from preserved knowledge while lacking the industrial and technological continuity that once made advanced systems ordinary.

## Runic Labyrinth Continuity Anchor

For **Runic Labyrinth**, the important stable point is:

**A meteor-fragment catastrophe caused a global technological setback, after which humanity rebuilt from surviving knowledge rather than continuing uninterrupted technological progress.**

Exact calendar years may be revised later without removing that core event from the game's historical foundation.

## Unresolved Material

The archive has not reconciled the following:

- whether M2 itself, its fragments, or an external force caused its orbital acceleration
- whether the fragments contained Chronolith, Chronolith Resonant material, or unrelated extraterrestrial matter
- the exact physical mechanism connecting deep impacts, volcanic activity, and the worldwide electromagnetic failure
- whether later groups intentionally exaggerated the role of militias in destroying surviving machines
- whether all regions of Earth experienced the technological collapse equally
