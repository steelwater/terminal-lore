# Horrox System

Astronomical designation: **m.471**

**Horrox is Planet m.471.**

The planet was discovered by astronomer Horaccio Roxas using the Star Terminal.

> **NULL ARCHIVIST // DATING DISCREPANCY**  
> Surviving records place the Star Terminal discovery in 1992, 2016, and—if the public announcement is treated as the discovery event—2020. These dates represent different surviving accounts and levels of public knowledge. The archive does not yet declare one date definitive.

## Planet m.471

Rocky exoplanet several light-years from Earth.

Known features:

- mineral-rich crust
- multiple moons
- no confirmed biological life

One recovered chronology says a 2029 probe reached Planet m.471 in 2036 after a seven-year journey and confirmed two moons and extensive mineral resources.

## Early Exploration Infrastructure

Recovered records describe the following expansion sequence:

- **2021** — LightTech funds a probe project to explore Planet m.471.
- **2029-08-14** — an unnamed Horrox probe launches and deploys relay beacons along its route.
- **2036-08-14** — the probe reaches Planet m.471 and reports mineral wealth, two moons, and no detected planetary life.
- **2039-09-06** — a satellite platform is established in orbit around Planet m.471.
- **2056** — a larger orbital station is built to support mining and operations around the system.

These dates remain `draft` chronology and may be revised as other records are reconciled.

## Minerva

Minerva was selected as the first mining site.

Known features:

- Hydronium Crystallite / Hydronium ore deposits
- heavy meteor impact history
- extensive crater fields
- automated mining operations

A recovered chronology places the first LightTech mining station on Minerva on **2054-11-04**. The unmanned facility shipped ore to Mars.

On **2065-05-14**, Minerva stopped sending communications and ore shipments. Later probes failed to determine a confirmed cause. Some footage and speculation suggested alien activity, but no alien life was verified.

## Viajero Mission

Recovered records describe **Viajero** as the first crewed long-distance mission sent to investigate the Horrox/Minerva situation.

The crew began training in 2067 and launched in 2068. They were described as convicted felons serving life sentences who were offered freedom and a chance to live on Minerva.

The crew modified a Personnel HyperSpace Terminal (**pHT**) to interface with ship computers and the Horrox orbital station.

On **2073-07-06**, Viajero docked at Minerva, found indications consistent with possible alien activity, restarted the mining operation, and departed for the orbital station. The ship then malfunctioned near Planet m.471.

Earth lost contact on **2073-07-09**.

A later record says Viajero returned to contact on **2076-08-31**, docked at the Horrox orbital station, and warned Earth about M2's movement.

> **NULL ARCHIVIST // MISSING INTERVAL**  
> No surviving account in this source explains Viajero's condition or location between the 2073 loss of contact and the 2076 transmission. The gap is preserved as an unresolved event rather than repaired by assumption.

## M2

M2 is the second moon of Planet m.471.

Recovered chronology says its orbital speed began accelerating on **2076-08-23**. After several rotations, it left the Horrox system on a trajectory toward Earth.

### Leading Activation Hypothesis

Later LightTech analysis connects M2's acceleration to **Chronolith residue propagation beyond the Sol system**.

Chronolith residue was carried into the Horrox system through:

- long-range probes
- warp stabilization systems
- Minerva mining infrastructure

The same analysis records anomalous signal amplification, the loss of Minerva communications, M2's non-random orbital acceleration, and broader system destabilization after Chronolith contamination reached Horrox.

LightTech's conclusion was that **the Horrox system responded to Chronolith presence**.

The mechanism remains unresolved. The leading hypothesis is that a non-local intelligence or system can detect Chronolith excitation and may interpret it as intrusion, signal, contamination, or an activation request.

This means M2 may not have accelerated spontaneously. Its movement may have been one component of a larger response triggered by human Chronolith activity.

Earth attempted interception, but M2 later fragmented. Nuclear deflection moved some fragments away from Earth while others continued into the inner Solar System.

Fragments from M2 ultimately contributed to the catastrophe timeline and the technological collapse of Earth.
